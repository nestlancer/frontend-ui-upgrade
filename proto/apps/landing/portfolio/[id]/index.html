The problem

Four systems of record (Razorpay dashboard, core banking NEFT files, the loan-management suite, and Tally) meant settlement day ran on three people cross-checking spreadsheets. Discrepancies surfaced weeks late, during audits.

What we shipped

  • A single reconciliation ledger ingesting Razorpay webhooks, bank statement parsers (NEFT/UTR), and LMS entries — every transaction matched, flagged, or quarantined within 4 minutes.
  • Ops console with a verification queue: UTR look-up, partial match suggestions, one-click settlement approval, and an immutable audit trail.
  • CFO view: day-end summaries, aging buckets, and GST-ready invoices generated per settlement batch.
"Audit season used to be six weeks of archaeology. This year it was a folder of links." — CFO, client

Stack decisions that mattered

Postgres with advisory locks for matcher idempotency; a queue-per-source ingestion design so a bad bank file never stops webhook flow; and Playwright-contract tests against Razorpay's sandbox before every deploy.
